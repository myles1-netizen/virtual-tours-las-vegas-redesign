A home is the biggest line item, but the decision to buy it is rarely about the home alone. It's about the life that comes with it — the morning coffee spot, the pool on a July afternoon, the dog park down the street, the kids' school three blocks away. Yet most Las Vegas listings photograph the house and nothing else. That's leaving money on the table.

## Why community shots convert

When I shoot a residential listing, the photos that get sent around to family and friends — the ones that actually generate referrals — are almost never the kitchen. They're the lifestyle shots: the backyard at twilight, the community gate, the view from the upstairs window, the walking path behind the property.

Those images answer the question every out-of-state buyer is silently asking: "What's it like to live here?" Las Vegas gets a lot of relocation buyers — moving in from California, the Midwest, the East Coast. They've never set foot in Summerlin or Henderson. A photo of the community pool, the park, the golf course, the trail system — that's what lets them picture the move.

## What I include in a community shoot

On top of the standard HDR interior and exterior set, I'll shoot the neighborhood amenities that matter to this buyer pool:

- The community entrance and monument signage (curb appeal starts here)
- Pools, clubhouses, fitness centers, tennis and pickleball courts
- Nearby parks, playgrounds, and walking or biking trails
- The view corridors — what you see from the property or the common areas
- Golf course frontage and water features

For new-build communities, this matters even more. When I worked with Lennar — one of the top-three US homebuilders, and I was their sole Matterport provider in this market — the community photography wasn't an afterthought. It was the lead. Buyers were choosing the lifestyle first and the floorplan second.

## The drone angle on community

Community context is where drone earns its keep more than anywhere else. A single aerial that shows the home in relation to the park, the school, the retail — that one frame can sell a relocation buyer before they ever tour the house. I'm FAA Part 107 certified, so every aerial is legal and insured.

## It's all one visit

Community photography doesn't have to be a separate booking. I fold it into the standard residential shoot so you get interior, exterior, twilight, and community coverage in a single trip — delivered in 24 to 48 hours.

A listing that shows only the house is selling half the product. The community is the other half — and in Las Vegas, where the master-planned lifestyle is the whole pitch, it's often the bigger half.
