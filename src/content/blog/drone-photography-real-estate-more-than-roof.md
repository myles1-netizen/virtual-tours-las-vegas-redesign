The first thing most agents ask for with drone is "a shot straight down at the roof." I understand the instinct — it shows the footprint of the house. But a roof shot is the least useful image in the whole gallery, and it's usually the only aerial image the listing ends up with. That's a missed opportunity.

## What a roof shot actually tells a buyer

Almost nothing useful. "Yes, the house has a roof. Yes, it is roughly this shape." A buyer already assumes both. What they actually want to know from an aerial is the stuff they can't see from the curb: the size of the lot, where the pool sits, how the backyard connects to the neighbors, what's down the street.

## The drone shots that earn the booking

The aerials that move buyers are about context. A flight that rises from the front door and pans out to show the whole street and the neighborhood. A shot that reveals the proximity to a park, a school, a golf course, or — here in the valley — the mountain backdrop. For a listing in Summerlin, an aerial showing the Spring Mountains framing the community sells the lifestyle as much as the house.

For acreage and land listings, drone isn't optional — it's the only way to show the shape and usability of the parcel. I fly the boundary so a buyer can see where the buildable area is, where the slope falls off, where the access road comes in.

## The legal part agents skip

You cannot legally fly a drone for compensation in the US without an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate. A lot of the "drone guys" offering cheap aerials on marketplace don't have one, and the MLS is increasingly strict about it — plus an unlicensed operator flying over people and property is a liability that can land on the listing agent.

I'm FAA Part 107 certified, insured, and I've been flying real estate aerials in Las Vegas for years. Every flight is logged and legal.

## Drone isn't just for the hero photo

A good aerial sequence gives you content for the whole marketing campaign: the listing hero, social posts, the walk-through video opener, the email blast. One flight, a dozen usable assets.

## What it costs

Aerial stills run as an add-on to a photo package, and a stand-alone aerial shoot can be quoted on its own. For residential I usually bundle drone into the package so the listing has ground and air coverage in a single visit — one booking, one trip, everything delivered together in 24 to 48 hours.

The roof is the least interesting part of the property. Show them why they want to live there.
