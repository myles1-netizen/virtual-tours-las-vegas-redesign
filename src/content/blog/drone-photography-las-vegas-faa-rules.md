A good drone shot is the fastest way to show a buyer the lot, the setting, and how close the home sits to the things they care about — the Strip, a park, a school, the mountains. But here's the thing most agents don't realize: you can't legally just hire anyone with a drone to shoot a listing for money. And in Las Vegas, where you *can* fly is a whole other question.

## The FAA Part 107 requirement

If anyone is paid for drone imagery, that's commercial use under FAA rules, and it requires a Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate. No exceptions. I hold mine, and it means I've passed the aeronautical knowledge exam, understand airspace classification, weather, and crew resource management, and I operate under the regulations.

Why does it matter to you? Because the FAA has started going after illegal operators, and MLS and brokerages are increasingly asking for proof of certification. An uncertified shoot is a liability for your listing, and the fines are real. When you hire me, the paperwork's already done.

## Las Vegas airspace is controlled

Here's the harder part. Most of the Las Vegas Valley sits within controlled airspace around Harry Reid International Airport. That means I can't just launch and fly — every commercial flight here requires airspace authorization through the FAA's Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability (LAANC) system.

I request authorization before each shoot, and the approval defines exactly where and how high I can fly. Near the airport corridors, altitudes get restricted. Some areas near Nellis Air Force Base and the strip flight paths have additional limits. It's not a dealbreaker — I fly approved shoots all over the valley — but it's why a pilot who actually knows the local airspace matters.

## When drone earns its keep

Not every listing needs aerial. A condo downtown doesn't. But drone is worth it when the lot, the view, or the setting is the selling point: a pool and backyard in Summerlin, acreage in the northwest, a golf course community, or a property where showing proximity to a landmark closes the deal.

I add five drone photos to an HDR shoot as the "Neon Skyline" package for $250, or you can add drone a la carte. Flights are weather-dependent — desert heat over 105°F can ground a drone, and high wind is a no-go.
