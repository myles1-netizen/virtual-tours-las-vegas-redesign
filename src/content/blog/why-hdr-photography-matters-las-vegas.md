I've shot thousands of Las Vegas homes, from one-bedroom condos to mansions with indoor basketball gyms, and the one thing that separates a listing that gets clicked from one that gets scrolled past is the same every time: the photography. Specifically, HDR.

## What HDR actually means

HDR stands for High Dynamic Range. The idea is simple — a single photo can't hold detail in both a bright window view and a shadowed corner. My camera can't see the range of light your eye can, so it has to pick: expose for the room and blow out the windows to white, or expose for the windows and plunge the room into darkness.

HDR solves that. I take several exposures of the same frame — one bright enough to hold the view outside, one balanced for the room, one dark enough to protect the shadows — and blend them by hand into a single image. Clean windows, true colors, real shadow detail. Walls that look straight because I shot them level.

## Why Las Vegas demands it

We get over 300 days of sun a year here, and that desert light is brutal. A south-facing great room in Summerlin at noon has a 10-stop difference between the window and the back wall. A cell phone shot turns that window into a white rectangle and flattens the whole space. HDR is the difference between a buyer feeling the room and wondering what's behind the glare.

## It earns more showings

Buyers process images faster than they read words. A crooked, dim, or blown-out photo signals a home that's not cared for, and they move on. Straight walls, accurate color, and a window you can actually see through signal value. That's why I treat every shoot like a 24/7 open house — because to the buyer clicking through at midnight, that's exactly what it is.
