There's a reason nearly every luxury listing leads with a twilight shot. That warm interior glow against a deep blue sky is the single most eye-catching image you can put on MLS, and it stops the scroll on social in a way a daytime photo never will. I shoot twilight all over the Las Vegas Valley, and when it's the right property, it earns its keep every time.

## What twilight photography actually is

Twilight — or "blue hour" — is the brief window after the sun sets when the sky turns a saturated, deep blue but isn't fully dark yet. During those 20 to 30 minutes, every interior light reads as warm glow, pool and landscape lighting pops, and the whole home looks alive. It's dramatic without being gaudy.

The catch is that window is short. I have to be set up, pre-lit, and ready to fire the moment the sky is right. There's no second take that night. That's why twilight is a specialty — it's part timing, part prep, part knowing exactly which exposures to blend.

## When twilight earns its keep

Not every home needs it. A daytime HDR shoot is the right call for the vast majority of resale listings. Twilight earns its place when:

- The home has **great exterior architecture** — custom stonework, a dramatic roofline, a striking entry
- There's a **pool, spa, or outdoor living space** that lighting transforms at night
- The property has a **view** — a Strip skyline view from a MacDonald Highlands or Summerlin luxury home is the classic Las Vegas twilight shot
- It's a **price point** where buyers expect a magazine-quality lead image

For a luxury listing, that first photo is everything. A buyer scrolling 40 listings on their phone stops on the warm, glowing twilight shot. That's the entire job of the lead image.

## How it's priced

Twilight is an add-on to an HDR shoot at $100. Because the blue-hour window is tight, I build the rest of the shoot around it — interior and exterior HDR first, then the twilight exteriors as the sun goes down. It's not a separate trip; it's the close of a full shoot.

## The honest take

Twilight won't save a property that doesn't show well. But on the right home — one with architecture, light, and a view worth showing off — it's the difference between a listing that looks like every other and one that looks like the one. In the Las Vegas luxury market, where the bar is high and the buyers are discerning, that's worth $100 every time.

If your listing has the bones for it, ask me about twilight. I'll tell you straight whether it'll pay off.
