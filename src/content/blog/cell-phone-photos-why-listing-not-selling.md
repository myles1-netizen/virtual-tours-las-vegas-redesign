I'll be blunt, because I have this conversation every week: if your listing is sitting on the market and the photos are cell-phone shots, the photos are a big part of why it's sitting. I'm not saying that to sell you a shoot. I'm saying it because agents text me the same struggling listings over and over, and the common factor is almost always the imagery.

## What a phone shot actually signals

Buyers don't read a phone photo and think "bad camera." They think "uncared-for home." A dim, slightly crooked, blown-out photo of a living room reads as a home that hasn't been maintained — even when the home is in great shape. The brain fills in the rest: if the listing photos are sloppy, the HVAC is probably sloppy too. Click. Next listing.

That snap judgment happens in under a second, before the buyer has read a word of the description. You don't get a second chance to make the first impression, and on the MLS the photos are the first impression.

## The three things a phone can't do

First, it can't handle our Las Vegas light. That south-facing great room at noon has a ten-stop spread between the window and the back wall. The phone picks one — bright room with a white window, or visible window with a dark room. My HDR rig holds both.

Second, it can't stay sharp in low light. The phone raises its sensitivity and the image goes grainy. On a retina display that grain reads as low quality. My tripod-mounted, low-ISO exposures are sharp corner to corner.

Third, it can't keep walls straight. The phone is handheld and tilted, so vertical lines converge and the room looks like it's leaning. I shoot level and correct in post. Walls stand straight.

## What it's costing you

The cost isn't just showings — it's price. Listings with professional photography sell for more and sell faster. The Airbnb data backs this up on the rental side: professionally photographed listings earn measurably more. The same logic applies to sales. A listing that lingers because of weak photos usually ends up taking a price reduction that's many times the cost of a photo shoot.

## What a real shoot costs

Less than most agents assume. HDR stills start at $110 for 10 photos, 11 to 30 photos is $135, and it scales up from there by count. A full residential package — HDR, Matterport, drone, floor plan — is a fraction of one month's carrying cost on a stale listing. Everything is delivered MLS-ready in 24 to 48 hours.

## If you're not sure, do the test

Pull up your live listing on your phone next to a competitor's listing shot by a pro. Scroll both the way a buyer would. The difference is obvious — and so is which one you'd rather be.

Every interior in my [portfolio](/work/) is professional HDR. Compare it to your current listing photos and you'll see exactly what buyers are seeing.

The phone is a great tool for a lot of things. Selling a half-million-dollar home isn't one of them.
