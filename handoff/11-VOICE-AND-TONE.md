# Voice & Tone — Writing as Mike

The client asked for first-person tone since Mike is a sole photographer, not a company. This is pulled from Mike-specific context already established for this business (not invented for this handoff):

## Core facts about voice

- **Mike is the business.** He is NOT a corporation, NOT a team — one person. Never use "we" anywhere on the site (headers, body copy, meta descriptions, buttons, error messages, everywhere). Use "I" when speaking as him, "you" when addressing the visitor.
- **Style:** corporate casual — professional but relaxed, not stiff, not corporate-speak. Direct. No fluff, no "Great question!"-style filler, no padding with buzzwords ("synergy," "leverage," "ecosystem," etc. — these have been explicitly flagged as things to never use).
- **Sarcastic at times, when it fits** — a little personality is fine and matches how he actually talks, but read the room; a pricing page or credentials section should stay mostly straightforward, while an About page or FAQ answer has more room for a dry aside.
- **SEO-aware but not SEO-obsessed** — natural language that happens to include real local-search terms (Las Vegas, Henderson, Matterport, etc.) because that's honestly how he'd describe his own work, not keyword-stuffed copy for its own sake (see `01-PROJECT-BRIEF.md` for a concrete example of what NOT to do — the current site's 140-character keyword-stuffed title tag).
- **Confident, not boastful.** He knows his work is good and says so plainly, without hedging ("might," "could potentially") but also without over-claiming (see `07-LEGAL-ACCURACY-GUIDELINES.md` on not inflating third-party relationships).

## Concrete rewrite guidance

- Headlines and CTAs: short, direct, first person or direct-address ("Book your shoot," "Call me," "Here's what I charge" rather than "Contact Us" or "Our Services")
- Body copy: conversational sentence rhythm, contractions are fine and expected ("I don't," "here's," "you'll"), avoid em-dash-as-clause-connector overuse (see `03-LESSONS-LEARNED.md` item 10 — this was already identified as the main "sounds like AI" tell and fixed once; don't reintroduce it)
- Credentials/certifications: state plainly, don't oversell (FAA Part 107, Google Trusted Photographer, licensed Nevada real estate agent since 2006 — these are already impressive on their own, no need to dress them up)
- When something's genuinely a strength worth some edge (e.g. comparing to cheaper competitors, or explaining why DIY/phone photos hurt a listing), it's fine to be a little pointed about it — that matches his actual voice, per direct guidance already established for this business ("Cheaper gets you a file. [His work] gets your listing sold faster — and that's what actually matters" is the kind of directness that fits).

## What to avoid

- "We" — anywhere, ever
- Generic AI-marketing-copy patterns: "Discover how...", "Elevate your...", "Transform your...", "Unlock the power of..." — these show up on the CURRENT site (see `content-audit/homepage-*.md` for an example — a blog teaser literally reads "Transform Your Listings with Strategic Media... Elevate your marketing strategy...") and read as generic/impersonal, the opposite of what a rewrite should produce
- Over-formality — this isn't a corporate law firm's site
- Apologetic or hedge-y language about pricing or capability — he's confident about what he charges and what he delivers
